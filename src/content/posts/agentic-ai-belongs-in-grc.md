Most GRC work isn't hard. It's heavy. Evidence collection, mapping controls across frameworks, reconciling inventories, chasing status. That weight is exactly where agentic tools earn their keep.

## Find the bottleneck before you pick the tool

When I partner with engineering on AI tooling, the first meeting isn't about models. It's about where work actually stalls. Usually it's a handoff: a form nobody owns, a spreadsheet that has to be rebuilt every week, a question that only one person can answer.

Automate that handoff and the whole workflow speeds up.

## The data is the product

An agent is only as good as what it's grounded on. Curating the datasets, meaning the right policies, the current control mappings, the approved language, is most of the work. It's also the part that protects you when someone asks where an answer came from.

## Keep accountability human

An AI can draft a System Security Plan section. It cannot own it. In regulated environments every artifact needs a person who can stand behind it, and the tooling should make that person faster, not invisible.

## What I'd tell a team starting out

Pick one painful, repetitive workflow. Measure how long it takes today. Ground an assistant on a small, trusted dataset. Keep a human approval step. Then measure again.
