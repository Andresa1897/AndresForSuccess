When Log4j hit, I was on the risk team at Walmart. The scale was the obvious problem: more than a million assets across on-prem, Azure and GCP. The less obvious problem was noise. Everyone needed answers at once.

## Triage is a decision, not a feeling

We had more than fifty business-critical artifacts to remediate. The only way through was to rank by business consequence and work the list, out loud, so every team knew where they stood.

## Calm is borrowed from practice

Years earlier I competed nationally in Call of Duty. Elimination brackets are a strange training ground for incident response, but they teach the same thing: when every mistake is visible, you slow your thinking down instead of speeding it up.

## Write down what you'd do next time

The best thing a team can do after an incident is turn what worked into a playbook while the memory is fresh. The next zero-day won't look like Log4j, but the coordination will.
